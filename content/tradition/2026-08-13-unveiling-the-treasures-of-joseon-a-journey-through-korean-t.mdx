Korean traditional culture is a treasure trove of rich history, vibrant colors, and profound philosophy. At the heart of this heritage lies the Joseon dynasty, which ruled Korea from 1392 to 1910. During this period, the royal court and nobility developed a distinct culture that emphasized etiquette, morality, and aesthetics.

One of the most iconic symbols of Korean traditional culture is the **hanbok**, a beautifully embroidered garment worn on special occasions such as weddings, holidays, and ancestral rites. The hanbok is not only a stunning piece of clothing but also a reflection of the wearer's social status, with intricate designs and colors signifying rank and position.

To experience the grandeur of the Joseon dynasty, visitors can explore the **Gyeongbokgung Palace**, the largest and most iconic of the five royal palaces in Seoul. This majestic palace is a must-visit destination, with its impressive architecture, serene gardens, and fascinating museum exhibits. Another notable palace is the **Changdeokgung Palace**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site renowned for its breathtaking beauty and historic significance.

Ancestral rites, known as **jesa**, are an essential part of Korean traditional culture, where families pay respects to their ancestors through elaborate ceremonies and offerings. These rituals are typically performed during the **Chuseok** harvest festival, which celebrates the bounty of the land and the unity of family.

For those interested in delving deeper into Korean traditional culture, there are several excellent resources available. The **Korean Cultural Heritage Administration** offers a wealth of information on Korea's cultural treasures, including palaces, temples, and festivals. The **National Museum of Korea** is another invaluable resource, with its vast collections of artifacts, artworks, and exhibits showcasing Korea's rich history and culture.

To get a hands-on experience of Korean traditional culture, visitors can try on a hanbok at a traditional tea house, such as the **O Sulloc Tea House**, or take a class in traditional Korean crafts, such as pottery or embroidery, at the **Korea House**.
