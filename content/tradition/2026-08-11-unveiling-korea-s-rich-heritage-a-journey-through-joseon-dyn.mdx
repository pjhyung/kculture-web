Korean traditional culture is a treasure trove of history, philosophy, and artistry. At its core lies the Joseon dynasty, which ruled Korea from 1392 to 1910. This period is often regarded as the golden age of Korean culture, marked by significant advancements in literature, music, and visual arts.

One of the most iconic symbols of Korean traditional culture is the **hanbok**, a beautifully crafted traditional garment worn on special occasions. Characterized by its vibrant colors and elegant designs, the hanbok is a testament to Korea's rich textile heritage. Visitors can experience the beauty of hanbok by attending a traditional Korean wedding or by trying one on at a hanbok rental shop in Seoul.

Korea's **royal palaces** are another window into the country's fascinating past. The Gyeongbokgung Palace, built in 1395, is the largest and most iconic of the five palaces, showcasing the grandeur and majesty of the Joseon dynasty. The Changdeokgung Palace, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is renowned for its stunning architecture and beautiful gardens.

Ancestral rites, known as **jesa**, play a significant role in Korean traditional culture. These rituals are performed to honor one's ancestors and pay respects to the deceased. The jesa ceremony typically involves the preparation of traditional food, drink, and incense, which are offered to the ancestors as a sign of gratitude and respect.

For foreigners interested in experiencing Korean traditional culture, there are several recommendations:
  * Visit the **National Museum of Korea** to learn about Korea's history and culture
  * Watch a performance of **'Noraebang'**, a traditional Korean music and dance show
  * Try on a hanbok at **Hanboknam**, a popular hanbok rental shop in Seoul
