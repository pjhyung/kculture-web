Korean traditional culture is a rich and vibrant tapestry, woven from centuries of history, philosophy, and art. At the heart of this heritage lies the Joseon dynasty, which ruled Korea from 1392 to 1910. During this period, the royal court and aristocracy developed a unique and refined culture that continues to inspire and influence modern Korean society.

One of the most iconic symbols of Korean traditional culture is the hanbok, a beautifully crafted garment that reflects the wearer's social status, occupation, and marital status. The hanbok is characterized by its elegant lines, vibrant colors, and intricate embroidery, making it a true masterpiece of Korean design. Visitors to Korea can experience the beauty of hanbok firsthand by attending a traditional tea ceremony or renting a hanbok to wear while exploring the country's historic sites.

Korea's royal palaces are another testament to the grandeur and majesty of the Joseon dynasty. The most famous of these palaces is Gyeongbokgung, which was built in 1395 and served as the main palace of the Joseon kingdom. This magnificent structure is a must-visit destination for anyone interested in Korean history and culture, with its stunning architecture, beautiful gardens, and world-class museums.

Ancestral rites are an essential part of Korean traditional culture, and are still practiced today by many Koreans. These rites are a way of honoring one's ancestors and showing respect for the past, and typically involve the preparation of traditional foods, the burning of incense, and the performance of rituals and ceremonies. Visitors to Korea can learn more about ancestral rites by attending a traditional ceremony or visiting the **National Folk Museum of Korea**, which offers exhibits and interactive displays on this and other aspects of Korean culture.

For those seeking to delve deeper into Korean traditional culture, there are many resources available. The **Korean Cultural Heritage Administration** offers a wealth of information on Korea's historic sites, cultural festivals, and traditional arts. The **'Hanbok: A New Vision'** exhibition at the **National Museum of Korea** is also a great place to learn about the history and significance of hanbok. Additionally, visitors can try **Kookje Hanbok**, a high-end hanbok rental service that offers a wide range of traditional garments for special occasions.
