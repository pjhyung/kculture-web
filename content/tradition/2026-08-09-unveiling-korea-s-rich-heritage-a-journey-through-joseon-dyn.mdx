Korea's traditional culture is a treasure trove of vibrant history, breathtaking architecture, and beautiful clothing. At the heart of this rich heritage lies the Joseon dynasty, which ruled Korea from 1392 to 1910. During this period, the kingdom flourished, and its culture became distinctly Korean.

One of the most iconic symbols of Korean traditional culture is the **hanbok**, a traditional Korean garment that has been worn for centuries. The hanbok is characterized by its elegant design, vibrant colors, and intricate patterns. It is typically worn on special occasions such as weddings, holidays, and traditional festivals.

Korea's royal palaces are another testament to the country's rich cultural heritage. The **Gyeongbokgung Palace**, built in 1395, is the largest and most iconic palace in Korea. It is a must-visit destination for anyone interested in Korean history and culture. The palace is surrounded by beautiful gardens and features stunning architecture that showcases the kingdom's grandeur.

Ancestral rites are an essential part of Korean traditional culture. The **Jongmyo Shrine**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is a sacred place where Koreans pay respects to their ancestors. The shrine is dedicated to the kings and queens of the Joseon dynasty and is an important symbol of Korea's cultural heritage.

For those interested in exploring Korean traditional culture, there are several resources available. The **Korean Folk Village** in Yongin is a great place to experience Korea's traditional way of life. The village features traditional Korean houses, gardens, and shops, and offers a range of cultural activities such as traditional crafts and performances.

In addition, the **National Museum of Korea** in Seoul is a treasure trove of Korean history and culture. The museum features a vast collection of artifacts, including traditional Korean clothing, jewelry, and artwork.

To learn more about Korean traditional culture, readers can check out **'Korean Folk Customs'** by the Korea Craft and Culture Association, which provides an in-depth look at Korea's traditional customs and practices. They can also watch **'The Emperor: Owner of the Mask'**, a historical drama that explores the life of a Joseon dynasty king.

For a unique experience, visitors can try on traditional hanbok at **Kyobo Book Centre** in Seoul, which offers hanbok rental services and photo opportunities.
