Korean traditional culture is a treasure trove of vibrant history, stunning architecture, and timeless customs. At the heart of this rich heritage lies the Joseon dynasty, which ruled Korea from 1392 to 1910. During this period, the Joseon people developed a unique and distinctive culture that continues to inspire and fascinate people around the world.

One of the most iconic symbols of Korean traditional culture is the hanbok, a beautifully crafted garment that has been worn for centuries on special occasions such as weddings, holidays, and ancestral rites. The hanbok is renowned for its elegant design, vibrant colors, and intricate embroidery, making it a true masterpiece of Korean craftsmanship.

For those interested in exploring Korean traditional culture, a visit to one of the five royal palaces in Seoul is a must. Gyeongbokgung Palace, built in 1395, is the largest and most iconic of these palaces, showcasing the grandeur and majesty of the Joseon dynasty. Visitors can wander through the palace's stunning courtyards, halls, and gardens, gaining a glimpse into the lives of Korea's royal ancestors.

Ancestral rites, known as 'jesa', play a significant role in Korean traditional culture, with many Koreans continuing to honor their ancestors through rituals and ceremonies. The National Museum of Korea in Seoul offers a fascinating insight into the history and significance of these rites, with a collection of artifacts and exhibits that highlight the importance of ancestral worship in Korean society.
