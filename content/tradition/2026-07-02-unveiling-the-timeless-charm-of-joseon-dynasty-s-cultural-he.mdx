Korean traditional culture is a treasure trove of vibrant history, stunning architecture, and profound philosophy. At the heart of this heritage lies the Joseon dynasty, which ruled Korea from 1392 to 1910. During this period, the Joseon people developed a unique and fascinating culture that continues to captivate audiences worldwide.

One of the most iconic symbols of Korean traditional culture is the **hanbok**, a beautifully crafted garment characterized by its elegant design and intricate patterns. The hanbok is not just a piece of clothing, but a reflection of the wearer's social status, marital status, and occupation. Visitors to Korea can experience the beauty of hanbok by trying one on at a traditional tea house or renting one for a special occasion.

The **royal palaces** of Joseon dynasty are another must-visit destination for anyone interested in Korean culture. The most famous palace, **Gyeongbokgung**, is a breathtaking example of traditional Korean architecture, with its majestic halls, serene gardens, and impressive stone walls. Visitors can explore the palace grounds, watch the changing of the guard ceremony, and learn about the history of the Joseon dynasty at the on-site museum.

Ancestral rites, known as **jesa**, are an essential part of Korean traditional culture. These rituals are performed to honor one's ancestors and pay respects to their memory. Visitors can learn about jesa by attending a traditional Korean funeral or visiting the **National Folk Museum of Korea**, which offers interactive exhibits and workshops on ancestral rites.

For those interested in exploring Korean traditional culture, we recommend:
* Visiting the **Bukchon Hanok Village** in Seoul, a charming neighborhood filled with traditional Korean houses and tea houses
* Watching the **Korean traditional music and dance performance** at the **National Theater of Korea**
* Trying on a hanbok at **Hanboknam**, a popular hanbok rental shop in Seoul
