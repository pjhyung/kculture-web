Korean mythology is a fascinating realm of legends, folklore, and supernatural creatures that have been passed down through generations. At the heart of Korean mythology are the dokkaebi, mischievous goblins known for their love of nature, music, and dance. These goblins are often depicted as short, muscular creatures with a fondness for playing tricks on humans.

Another iconic creature in Korean mythology is the gumiho, a nine-tailed fox with magical powers. According to legend, the gumiho can transform into a beautiful woman, seducing men with its charms. However, its true form is that of a powerful, nine-tailed fox with the ability to control the forces of nature.

Korean folklore tales are filled with stories of these supernatural creatures, as well as others like the dragon king, Yongwang, and the heavenly maiden, Cheonnyeon. These stories have been passed down through oral tradition and have been immortalized in classic Korean literature, such as the *Samguk Yusa* and *The Tale of Hong Gildong*.

For those interested in exploring Korean mythology and folklore, there are several great resources available. The book *Korean Folk Tales* by Zong In-Sob is a great introduction to the subject, offering a collection of classic tales that showcase the diversity and richness of Korean folklore. The Korean Folk Village in Yongin, Gyeonggi Province, is also a must-visit destination, offering a glimpse into traditional Korean culture and a chance to see dokkaebi and gumiho come to life in the form of costumes and performances.

To experience the magic of Korean mythology firsthand, consider watching the Korean drama *My Roommate is a Gumiho*, which tells the story of a college student who falls in love with a gumiho. You can also try playing the popular Korean video game *MapleStory*, which features dokkaebi and other creatures from Korean mythology as characters.
