Korean mythology is a treasure trove of fascinating creatures, legends, and tales that have been passed down through generations. At the heart of this rich cultural heritage are the dokkaebi, mischievous goblins known for their love of mischief and mayhem. These quick-witted creatures are often depicted as short, grotesque beings with a penchant for playing tricks on humans.

Another iconic figure in Korean mythology is the gumiho, a nine-tailed fox with shape-shifting abilities and supernatural powers. According to legend, the gumiho can take on various forms, from a beautiful woman to a powerful fox, and is often associated with good fortune and prosperity.

Korean folklore is filled with stories of these creatures, as well as other mythical beings like the dragon-like yong and the celestial maiden, Cheonnyeon. These tales have been immortalized in various forms of Korean art, literature, and film, including the classic novel *The Tale of Hong Gildong* and the popular TV drama *My Love from the Star*.

For those interested in exploring Korean mythology further, there are several recommended resources. The *Korean Folk Village* in Yongin, Gyeonggi Province, is a great place to start, offering a glimpse into traditional Korean life and culture. The *National Museum of Korea* in Seoul is another must-visit destination, with its extensive collection of artifacts and exhibits on Korean mythology and folklore.

In addition, the novel *The Hen Who Dreamed She Could Fly* by Sun-mi Hwang is a beautifully written tale that draws on Korean mythological themes and motifs. For a more immersive experience, try watching the Korean fantasy film *The Shape of Water*, which features a gumiho as one of its main characters.

Whether you're a longtime fan of Korean culture or just starting to explore its rich heritage, delving into Korean mythology is sure to be a rewarding and fascinating experience.
