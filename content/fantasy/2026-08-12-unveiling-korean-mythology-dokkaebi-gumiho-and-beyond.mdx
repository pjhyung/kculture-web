Korean mythology is a fascinating world of supernatural creatures, legends, and folklore. At its core are the dokkaebi, mischievous goblins known for their love of troublemaking and penchant for magic. These mythical beings are often depicted as short, grotesque creatures with a fondness for human belongings.

Another iconic figure in Korean mythology is the gumiho, a nine-tailed fox with shape-shifting abilities. According to legend, the gumiho can take on various forms, from a beautiful woman to a powerful fox. With its intelligence, cunning, and magical powers, the gumiho is a revered and feared creature in Korean folklore.

Korean folklore tales are filled with stories of these supernatural beings, as well as other mythical creatures like the bulgasari, a mythical dog said to have the power to sense and ward off evil spirits. These tales have been passed down through generations, often serving as cautionary stories or explanations for natural phenomena.

For those interested in delving deeper into Korean mythology, there are several resources available. The book *Korean Folk Tales* by Zong In-Sob is a great starting point, offering a collection of traditional stories that showcase the country's rich cultural heritage. Visitors to Korea can also explore the **National Folk Museum of Korea** in Seoul, which features exhibits on Korean folklore and mythology.

To experience Korean mythology firsthand, consider watching the popular Korean drama *My Love From the Star*, which features a gumiho as one of its main characters. You can also try **KakaoTalk**, a popular Korean messaging app that features dokkaebi and gumiho characters as stickers and emojis.

In conclusion, Korean mythology is a captivating world of legendary creatures, folklore, and supernatural beings. With its rich history and cultural significance, it's an essential part of Korean heritage that continues to inspire and fascinate people around the world.
