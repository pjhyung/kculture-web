Korean daily life is a unique blend of traditional and modern culture. From bustling streets to peaceful temples, Korea has something to offer for everyone. One of the most distinctive aspects of Korean culture is its street food scene. Popular dishes like *tteokbokki* (spicy rice cakes) and *hotteok* (sweet pancakes) can be found at street stalls and markets throughout the country.

Convenience store culture is also a significant part of Korean daily life. Stores like 7-Eleven, CU, and GS25 are open 24/7, offering a wide range of products, from snacks and beverages to toiletries and cosmetics. These stores often have a seating area where customers can rest and enjoy their purchases.

For foreigners interested in Korean culture, learning a few basic Korean language expressions can go a long way. Phrases like *annyeonghaseyo* (hello), *gamsahamnida* (thank you), and *joheunhamnida* (you're welcome) are essential for interacting with locals. The Korean government also offers language classes and cultural programs for foreigners, such as the *King Sejong Institute*, which provides free Korean language lessons and cultural activities.

Korea is also famous for its *K-dramas* (Korean dramas) and *K-pop* (Korean pop music). Fans of these genres can visit the *SMTOWN Coex Artium*, a theme park dedicated to K-pop, or attend a concert at the *Olympic Gymnastics Arena*.

In terms of products, Korea is known for its high-quality skincare and beauty products. Brands like *Innisfree* and *Laneige* offer a wide range of products, from moisturizers to makeup.
