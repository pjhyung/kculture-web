Korean daily life is a fascinating blend of traditional and modern practices. In the morning, Koreans often start their day with a warm bowl of **juk** (rice porridge) or **tteok** (rice cake) from a street vendor. The Korean language is also an integral part of daily life, with common expressions like **annyeonghaseyo** (hello) and **gamsahamnida** (thank you) used frequently.

Street food is a staple of Korean culture, with popular dishes like **tteokbokki** (spicy rice cakes) and **hotteok** (sweet pancakes) available at almost every corner. For a unique experience, visit **Myeong-dong Shopping District** in Seoul, where you can find a variety of street food vendors and try **kimbap** (Korean-style sushi) or **mandu** (Korean dumplings).

Convenience store culture is also thriving in Korea, with chains like **7-Eleven** and **CU** offering a wide range of products, from snacks and drinks to cosmetics and clothing. Koreans often rely on these stores for quick meals, with popular items like **ramyeon** (instant noodles) and **kimbap** available 24/7.

For foreigners interested in Korean culture, learning a few basic language expressions can go a long way. Try using **saranghae** (I love you) or **joheunhamnida** (excuse me) to connect with the locals. You can also watch Korean dramas like **Crash Landing on You** or **Vagabond** to get a glimpse into Korean daily life and culture.

When visiting Korea, be sure to try some of the local products, such as **Pepero** (a popular Korean snack) or **Bibigo** (a brand of Korean chili paste). You can find these products at convenience stores or supermarkets like **Lotte Mart**.
